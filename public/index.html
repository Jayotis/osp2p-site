A social network that runs from your home.

Open Social P2P is a home social network. Your identity, posts and media live on a node you control, while your phone feels like a normal social app. No single company holds every account or controls one global engagement feed.

START WITH WHAT YOU HAVE

You don't need to buy a server.

OSP is designed so your long-running node can eventually live on inexpensive, low-power hardware at home. Product Alpha is being built and tested on ordinary computers first; Raspberry Pi-class SBC performance is a target we measure, not a promise we pretend is already proven.

THE SIGNATURE FEATURE

See the network instead of hiding it.

Universe is OSP's zoomable view of the social network. Start at your own star, explore public connections, discover people and content, and zoom from individual relationships out toward larger communities.

The map is built from information your own node already legitimately knows. Moving around it does not secretly query other people just to fill the screen.

At large scale, the node summarizes the sky into stable levels of detail instead of asking a phone to render millions of people at once.

HOW OSP WORKS

A familiar app on top of a network that isn't owned by one platform.

1

Run your node

Your computer owns the long-lived identity and stores your posts and media. Start on the machine you already have.

2

Pair your phone

The mobile app is the everyday interface for posting, following, replying and exploring.

3

Meet the network

Nodes discover one another, exchange signed public information and build their own local view instead of asking one central service what the network is.

4

Share the load

Reshared public content can have verified copies served from more than one participating home, spreading storage and bandwidth across the network.

PRIVACY BY ARCHITECTURE

Home-hosted doesn't mean privacy claims get a free pass.

OSP separates people from machines and deliberately limits what the Universe may display. It does not expose IP addresses, relay addresses, private viewing history, hidden peer scores or guessed location.

Public discovery and public media still involve real network tradeoffs. OSP documents those tradeoffs rather than promising impossible anonymity, and it does not depend on a centralized company learning what you watch just to rank a corporate feed.

CURRENT STATUS
