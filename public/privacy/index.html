PRIVACY

Own more of the network without pretending the internet is invisible.

OSP moves identity, content and much of discovery back to hardware you control, while documenting the privacy tradeoffs that still exist on a public network.

Your node owns your social state

Your identity, posts, media, follows, local search index and network state live on your node rather than existing only inside a platform account.

Search can stay local

Public discovery material can be brought to your node and searched there. That avoids requiring one central search service to receive every query you type.

Universe has an allowlist

The map deliberately excludes data such as IP addresses, relay addresses, private viewing history, hidden peer scores, guessed geography and private machine-person associations. Navigation itself is not permission to fetch more social data.

Public still means public

Public posts and public media are meant to be discoverable. Network participants and relays can observe metadata appropriate to the paths they participate in. OSP does not claim to be an anonymity network.

No corporate feed owner

OSP can use algorithms for clustering, search, peer selection and discovery. The distinction is that there is no single corporation controlling one global feed and optimizing it for advertising or engagement across the whole network.

Private content comes later

Product Alpha focuses on public content. Private groups and private peer-to-peer content require their own validated design rather than being bolted onto the public system with vague privacy claims.

Privacy by architecture

OSP's privacy claims are intentionally narrower than “anonymous” or “no servers.” The project documents what is public, what stays local and what network infrastructure can still learn.
