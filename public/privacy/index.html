PRIVACY POLICY

Open Social P2P Privacy Policy

OSP is designed so most of your social data lives on hardware you control instead of in a central account database. This policy explains what the OSP app and network access, what can leave your device or home node, what is public, and what the OSP project itself may receive.

Effective and last updated: November 16, 2026

1. Who this policy covers

This Privacy Policy applies to the official Open Social P2P (OSP) mobile apps, the official OSP node software, project-operated OSP infrastructure used during Product Alpha, osp2p.com, and support sent to support@osp2p.com.

OSP is open-source peer-to-peer software. Other people can run compatible nodes, directories, relays, modified software or other services that are not operated by the OSP project. Their handling of data is outside this policy.

2. The important difference: there is no central OSP account

Joining OSP creates your identity on your own node. The phone pairs with that node; it does not create a separate cloud account with OSP. The project does not operate a central username/password database containing every user.

Your own node stores your identity, profile, posts, media, follows, local search information, settings and network state. Other OSP nodes may receive public information when you publish, follow, reply, reshare, like, use discovery or participate in peer-to-peer distribution.

3. Information the app accesses

Public profile and social content

When you choose to publish, OSP may handle your cryptographic public identity, display name, profile image, profile summary, links you add, public posts, replies, likes, reshares and attached photos or videos. Public OSP content is intended to be visible to other people and may be copied to other participating nodes.

Photos and videos you choose

The mobile app accesses photos or videos only when you choose media for a profile or post. Selected media is sent to your own node. If you publish it publicly, the resulting media may be served to and stored by other participating OSP nodes as part of the peer-to-peer network.

Camera

The app uses the camera to scan pairing, invite and OSP QR codes. OSP does not intentionally send camera frames to the OSP project. You can paste supported codes instead of using the camera.

Device pairing information

The app creates a device key used to authenticate the phone to your own node. The key is kept in secure device storage. The app also reads a general device model name, such as “iPhone 12” or an Android manufacturer/model, so your own node can label the paired phone. This device label is not used for advertising or tracking by the OSP project.

Local app data and diagnostics

The app can keep a saved view of your feed, pairing information, unfinished uploads, pending deletes and diagnostic logs on your device. Diagnostic logs stay local unless you choose to share them, for example with a support or bug report.

Notifications

On Android, OSP can check your own node in the background and create local notifications about activity or an upload in progress. The current Product Alpha does not use a third-party advertising or analytics notification service for this. On iPhone, activity is currently shown when the app is opened; upload progress may be shown using iOS system features.

Information OSP does not currently request for its core Product Alpha features

OSP does not currently request your contacts, precise location, SMS messages, call logs, health data or payment-card information. The Product Alpha does not contain advertising SDKs or third-party analytics/crash-reporting SDKs used by the OSP project.

4. Public discovery and directories

Being listed in an OSP directory is opt-in. If you opt in, a directory may receive and republish your signed public profile and recent public posts so people who do not already follow you can discover you. Directories do not need your original media files to list those posts.

During Product Alpha, the project maintainer may operate an initial directory so new users can find people. The OSP protocol also allows independent people to run directories. A directory can omit or remove a listing, but it cannot change a valid signed post or profile without invalidating its signature.

Machine-level discovery also uses pseudonymous node or machine identifiers and availability information. OSP deliberately separates those machine identities from public social identities where the protocol permits.

5. Network information and peer-to-peer connections

OSP needs network information to connect devices and nodes. Depending on the path used, participating peers, relay operators, DNS infrastructure, internet providers or Tor infrastructure may process information such as IP addresses, node identifiers, connection times, routing information and byte counts.

A direct peer-to-peer connection can reveal the participants' IP addresses to each other. A relay can hide each peer's address from the other peer, while the relay operator can still see network metadata needed to carry the connection. OSP also uses Tor for some node-to-node messaging. These systems reduce some exposures but do not make all network activity anonymous.

The Product Alpha currently uses public iroh/n0 relay and DNS infrastructure for some connectivity. Those providers may process network metadata under their own policies.

6. Support, website and project communications

If you email support@osp2p.com, the OSP project receives the email address you use, your message and anything you attach. That information is used to respond to the request, investigate bugs or safety concerns, and maintain necessary support, abuse or security records.

osp2p.com is a static website and does not intentionally use advertising trackers or behavioural analytics. Its hosting provider may process ordinary web-request information such as IP address, browser information, request time and requested pages for delivery, reliability and security.

The project currently uses Cloudflare for website delivery and email routing, and GitHub for source code and optional public issue reports. Information you send directly to those services is also subject to their policies.

7. How information is used

OSP uses the information described above only as needed to provide the social network and project operations, including to:

  • pair your phone with your own node;
  • publish, retrieve and display profiles, posts, replies, likes, reshares and media;
  • find people and public content you have chosen to make discoverable;
  • route and verify peer-to-peer traffic;
  • protect the network from abuse and malformed or unauthorized requests;
  • provide support, investigate bug reports and respond to safety or security reports; and
  • operate and improve the Product Alpha.

OSP does not sell personal information and does not use OSP data to build advertising profiles or serve targeted advertising.

8. Who information can be shared with

Because OSP is a peer-to-peer social network, “sharing” is often the feature you asked the software to perform. Depending on what you do, information may be received by:

  • Your own OSP node, which receives requests and selected media from your paired phone.
  • Other OSP users and nodes, when you publish public content, interact publicly, follow people, or participate in media distribution.
  • Directories, if you choose to be publicly discoverable.
  • Relay, DNS, Tor and internet infrastructure, which processes the network metadata needed to establish or carry connections.
  • Cloudflare, for osp2p.com delivery and support-email routing.
  • GitHub, when you choose to use the public repository, issue tracker or GitHub security-reporting features.

OSP does not provide public social data to an advertising network or data broker on behalf of the Product Alpha.

9. Security

OSP uses cryptographic identities and signed social objects so nodes can verify who signed public information. Connections use encrypted transports where provided by the OSP protocol and its networking libraries. Public content is still public; encryption in transit does not make a public post confidential after it reaches another participant.

The phone's OSP device key is stored using secure storage provided by the operating system. Android uses the Android Keystore for the storage key, and iPhone uses Keychain storage restricted to that device. Pairing codes are temporary secrets and should be treated like passwords while they are valid.

No security design can guarantee absolute security. If you believe you found a vulnerability, use the private reporting route linked from the Support page rather than publishing credentials or exploit details.

10. Retention

Most OSP data is retained by your own node according to your choices and the software's local storage rules, rather than by a central OSP service.

  • Public posts and media: remain on your node and may remain on cooperating peers until deleted, evicted or no longer hosted under their local policies.
  • Directory listings: remain while you are opted in and are replaced or removed as the directory refreshes your public profile and recent posts.
  • Phone pairing and local app state: remain on the phone until you forget the node, clear the app's data or uninstall the app, subject to operating-system behaviour.
  • Paired-device authorization on your node: remains until the device is unpaired from the node.
  • Support and safety communications: may be retained as long as reasonably needed to answer the request, investigate abuse or security issues, maintain necessary records, or meet legal obligations, and are deleted when no longer needed for those purposes.

11. Your choices, deletion and privacy requests

OSP does not give you a central project account to delete. Your OSP identity is created and operated on your own node. You nevertheless have several ways to remove or limit data:

  • Delete your posts or replies: use OSP's delete controls. A signed deletion is sent through cooperating OSP nodes and those nodes stop presenting and serving the deleted post under the standard protocol.
  • Stop public directory discovery: turn off directory listing on your node. A later profile tells directories to remove you from their listings as they refresh.
  • Remove this phone's local OSP state: use “Forget this node,” clear the app's storage or uninstall it. To revoke a previously paired phone's authorization completely, also unpair that device on your node.
  • Remove data held by project-operated services: email support@osp2p.com with a privacy or deletion request. The project can delete or de-list information it controls, subject to legitimate security, legal or record-keeping needs.

Limits of deletion in a decentralized network: OSP can ask cooperating standard nodes to remove a signed post and stop serving its media, but the project cannot erase copies someone independently saved, copies held by modified/non-cooperating software, or a separate post by another account that happens to contain the same file. Public information already received by independent participants may therefore persist outside the project's control.

12. Consent and device permissions

OSP asks for operating-system permissions when a feature needs them, such as camera access for QR scanning, media access when you select a photo or video, and notification permission on supported Android versions. You can deny or later change operating-system permissions in your device settings, although the related feature may stop working.

You control whether to publish public content, opt in to directory listing, follow people, host or reshare public media, and send diagnostic information to support.

13. Children

The OSP Product Alpha is not directed to children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has provided personal information to a project-operated OSP service, contact support@osp2p.com so the project can review and remove information it controls where appropriate.

14. Changes to this policy

OSP is still in Product Alpha and its architecture may change. If a change materially affects what data the official app or project-operated services access, collect, use or share, this policy and the relevant App Store or Google Play disclosures will be updated. The effective date at the top of this page will change when the policy changes.
