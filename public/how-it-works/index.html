HOW IT WORKS

A normal social app on top of a network that runs from home.

The phone is the interface. Your home node owns the long-lived identity, stores your content and participates in the network.

The home node

Your node is the durable part of your OSP identity. It stores your posts, photos and videos, follows, local search data and network state. The goal is that an everyday node can eventually run on inexpensive, low-power hardware at home; you can start on a normal computer.

The phone

The mobile app is the everyday social experience: Home, Universe and You, with posting and Activity always reachable. The phone does not need to become an always-on server.

Signed social data

Public posts and profiles are signed by their authors. Media is content-addressed and verified, so another participating node can serve a copy without silently changing it.

Peer-to-peer distribution

When people deliberately reshare public content with hosting, verified copies can be served from more than one node. That lets distribution spread across participating homes instead of making one central platform pay every upload and hold every copy.

Discovery happens locally

OSP can receive public discovery material from directories, then search that material on the home node. The words typed into search do not need to be sent to a central search company.

Not “no infrastructure”

OSP still uses real infrastructure where the network needs it, including directories and relay paths. The architectural difference is that the network is not built around one corporation owning every identity, post, feed and media path.

Start with what you have

Product Alpha is being built on ordinary computers first. Cheap always-on SBC-class hardware is a target to validate, while creators can use stronger machines for heavier video workloads.
